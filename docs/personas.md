# Personas

1.Aysha

Situation and background: Aysha is a 28-year-old software engineer from India working full-time, which means she can only work on her applications late at night and has no way to call university offices during business hours. She wants to get into a top Master's program in Machine Learning and AI in Germany, but she is completely swamped trying to figure out certified copies, the APS certificate, and converting her CGPA.

 Goals:
	1.	Successfully get into a good ML program in Germany without having to quit her job.
	2.	Figure out the complex German prerequisites and grade conversion rules entirely on her own at night. 
	3.	Submit a flawless application package before the deadline despite her tight schedule.

Frustrations:
	1.	Trying to sort out paperwork like the APS certificate and official translation rules outside of office hours when no one is available to help.
	2.	The anxiety of calculating her CGPA into the German scale incorrectly and getting instantly rejected.
	3.	Wasting precious evening hours searching through messy, scattered university portals for basic requirements.

Technical skills:
Being a software engineer, she is very tech-savvy and comfortable using modern web tools and cloud platforms.

2.Albin (he is a first time applicant)

Situation and background: Albin is a 23-year-old recent bachelor graduate from India applying to study abroad for the very first time. He wants to pursue a Master's in Logistics in Germany, but everything about international admissions feels overwhelming since he is navigating it straight out of his undergraduate studies.

Goals:
	1.	Successfully submit his first-ever international application without making a silly paperwork mistake.
	2.	Understand how his bachelor grades map over to the German grading scale.
	3.	Receive clear, step-by-step guidance on what documents to provide.

Frustrations:
	1.	Panic over formatting rules, terrified that a minor file upload error will ruin his chances right before the deadline.
	2.	Getting completely confused by university portals that assume applicants already know how international credit transfer works.
	3.	Total radio silence after submitting, leaving him anxious and unable to plan his visa timeline.

Technical skills: 
He gets around fine using standard websites, mobile apps, and phone scanners, but he isn't used to complex administrative workflows.

3 Markus (The Reviewer)

Situation and background: Markus is a 42-year-old admissions reviewer in the international office of a German university, facing his absolute busiest weeks of the year when thousands of applications flood in at once. He is responsible for verifying foreign degrees and language levels under crushing pressure, handling 40 or more applications a day.

Goals:
	1.	Get through the massive application pile before the deadline without burning out or letting errors slip by.
	2.	Make admissions decisions that he can easily defend and justify if an applicant or supervisor asks why.
	3.	Spend less time on repetitive manual data checking and more time on complex cases.

Frustrations:
	1.	Severe mental fatigue from staring at dense, unformatted PDF files and doing repetitive lookups all day.
	2.	Dealing with vague or outdated guidelines that turn foreign degree checks into an agonizing chore.
	3.	The constant stress of looming deadlines and the fear of missing a fraudulent document in the rush.

Technical skills: He is comfortable with standard office software and internal databases, but prefers clean, intuitive screens that save him unnecessary clicks.

4 Sofia (The Administrator)

Situation and background: Sofia is a 38-year-old system administrator and university IT officer responsible for managing user accounts, role permissions, and updating the system's local knowledge base rules during peak admission cycles.

Goals:
	1.	Keep the platform running smoothly, securely, and free of technical bottlenecks.
	2.	Accurately manage user roles and permissions across applicant, reviewer, and admin tiers.
	3.	Quickly troubleshoot and resolve any configuration or file upload errors reported by staff.

Frustrations:
	1.	Malformed rule files or corrupted data packages breaking knowledge-base imports right before deadlines.
	2.	Manually tracking down permission mismatches or login lockouts for staff members.
	3.	Lack of clear system logs when background services fail to parse uploaded configurations.

Technical skills: Advanced system administrator proficient in server management, file parsing, database management, and role-based access control systems.
